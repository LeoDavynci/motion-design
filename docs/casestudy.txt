--- p2
01 · Context
Meeting people is easy .
Remembering them is hard.
At a conference
You meet dozens of people in a few 
hours. By day two the details blur: who 
does what, who you promised an intro 
to, who’s from your city.
In a new city
You meet people slowly over months, 
but there’s no network to place them 
in. You’re stuck at a name and a face.
In your phone
Contacts and LinkedIn keep a name 
and a job title. They don’t keep the 
story, or what you remember about 
them.
--- p3
Dunbar’s number
150Anthropologist Robin Dunbar proposed that people can 
comfortably keep stable relationships with about 150 
others, because of cognitive limits.
With new tech and AI,
why stop at 150? 150 you can keep in your head The rest of the people you’ve met
--- p4
02 · Who it’s for
T ech-native young adults 
who make a lot of 
connections and want to 
keep them.
Founders, grad students, new arrivals, people who go to 
events or are starting their lives in a new environment. 
Social, busy, and already using their phone for everything.
The problem: three gaps
Memory gap
Hard to remember everyone, and harder to 
remember their stories.
Follow-up gap
You forget what you promised and when you last 
talked.
Tooling gap
Contacts are too thin, social networks are public, 
CRMs are too heavy.

Process 4/6 · Design system
A design system where color carries meaning.
Soft Clean Mesh gradient Glass Blobs Textured Pastel
Moodboard keywords. Feedback on v1: deeper, more vibrant, don’t be afraid of darker 
tones.
HUE = HOW YOU KNOW THEM DEPTH = HOW WELL YOU KNOW THEM
Work Social Community Family & home Just met Getting to know Know well
One core / wash / deep per relationship. Avatars, mindmap dots and 
profile heroes all use it.
Each logged moment deepens the aura. Type: Geist, -4% on headings; 
Geist Mono for meta. (The serif was dropped.)
